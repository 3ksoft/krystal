Reviewer: Claude Opus 5
Date: 2026-08-03
Scope: full pack — `architecture.md`, ADA-0001…0007, `experiments/{block-cache,validation,matmul-wq4}.md`, `references.md`

---

## Overall assessment

This is an unusually disciplined architecture pack for a project at this stage. The status vocabulary is real (not decorative), the separation between ADA and experiment notes holds, and the two hardest things — **"Context State, not KV cache"** and **"cache failure must not change semantics"** — are both correctly identified as load-bearing. Most reviews of documents like this end up arguing about what should be deferred; here the deferral boundaries are largely drawn in the right places.

The problems are not in what is left open. They are in **three places where a decision already marked ACCEPTED is doing more work than its evidence supports**, and in **one economic claim the whole BlockStore thesis rests on that the pack never states, let alone quantifies**.

Specifically:

1. **ADA-0007's central invariant is already violated by both headline features** (depth-2 is not bit-exact; depth-5 has no exact path to fall back *to*). The invariant is stated as if it holds, and every downstream test strategy depends on it.
2. **The word `materialize` in §6 hides an O(context × layers) operation.** This is the "clean abstraction hiding an expensive implementation" case, and it inverts the value proposition of the deep BlockStore.
3. **Three documents each own one third of the same problem** — the decode submission window — and no document owns it whole. ADA-0003 calls it an invariant, ADA-0001 lists its consequences as "Negative", ADA-0005 treats it as an open risk.

Everything else below is refinement around a sound core.

**A note on numbers.** I've used the published LFM2 config (hidden 2048, ff 8192, 32 Q heads / 8 KV heads, head_dim 64, 16 layers, conv_L_cache 3). These reconstruct to **1.036 B non-embedding + 134 M tied embedding = 1.17 B**, matching the model card exactly, so I'm treating them as reliable — but verify the KV-head count against your own GGUF metadata before acting on the storage figures.

## 1. Architectural strengths worth preserving

**Context State as the primitive, not KV cache.** ADA-0002's insistence on this is correct and non-obvious, and it is the decision that will age best. Keep the terminology pressure on.

**The exactness classification exists at all.** Most projects discover the exact/approximate distinction after shipping. Having it in the vocabulary from revision 0.2 is the single highest-value thing in the pack — even though I argue below that its current *shape* is wrong.

**"Blocks are token sequences, not text."** ADA-0004's rejection of text-as-cache-identity is right, and the reasoning (boundary tokenization, tokenizer versions) is the correct reasoning, not a rationalization.

**Refusing to generalize to arbitrary architectures.** ADA-0002's "genericity is not itself a milestone" is the right posture, and the LFM2.5 hybrid is genuinely a better teacher than a plain transformer would have been — a pure-transformer reference model would have let you build a KV-cache abstraction that quietly doesn't generalize.

**Deferring the host language while naming the candidate.** DEFERRED-with-a-favorite is a more honest status than OPEN, and the reasoning ("porting experimental structure rather than stable semantics") is exactly right.

**WGSL as the retained shader representation.** This is the highest-leverage portability asset you have and it is already correctly frozen. Protect it (see §6.9).

**The depth-2 result itself.** Independently composable exact reuse at 12.9% of layers, with a bounded repair window, measured at 11–13% — the agreement between the structural prediction and the measurement is strong evidence the experiment was clean. That's worth more than the speedup.

## 2. Potentially incorrect assumptions

### 2.1 "Cache failure may reduce performance but must not silently change exact inference semantics" — **already false, twice** · **HIGH**

ADA-0004 invariant 2 and ADA-0007 both state this. Neither of your two implemented reuse paths satisfies it.

**Depth-2 is mathematically exact but not numerically exact.** The cached region was computed with a different matmul shape (`M` = block length, alone) than the same tokens would get in a full prefill (`M` = total prompt length). Different tile decomposition ⇒ different accumulation order ⇒ different rounding. With greedy argmax, a near-tie at any position flips a token, and after that the sequences diverge completely and permanently.

*Failure mode:* a bug report reading "same prompt gives different answers depending on whether the cache was warm." You will spend a day looking for a state-management bug that does not exist. Worse, "1:1 output on tested prompts" is currently your only oracle — it will hold until the first prompt with a near-tie, at which point it fails catastrophically rather than gracefully, and you will not be able to tell numeric drift from a genuine composition bug.

*Amendment:* restate the invariant as **mathematical** equivalence up to floating-point reassociation, and replace the string-equality oracle with a numeric one (see §8.1). This is a documentation change plus a test-methodology change; it does not require an architecture change, which is why it's HIGH and not BLOCKER.

**Depth-5 has no exact path to fall back to.** Recompute-on-miss for a depth-5 block does not reproduce the cached answer — it produces a *different, better* one. So "cache is an optimization" is not merely imprecise here, it is inverted: the cache is the *only* way to obtain the depth-5 semantics. Eviction is therefore a semantic event.

*Failure mode:* BlockStore evicts under pressure → the runtime "correctly" falls back to recompute → the answer changes → the change is attributed to model nondeterminism. Or the runtime doesn't fall back, silently serving a lower-quality answer with no signal.

### 2.2 Depth-5 is treated as a cache tier when it is a generation mode · **HIGH**

Follows directly from 2.1. `CachedRepresentation.exactnessClass` is the abstraction leak: it makes a **user-visible semantic choice** look like a **storage attribute**. The moment `exactnessClass` is a field on a cache object, every layer below the API is entitled to change it — eviction policy, GC, residency tiering, memory pressure — and each of those is now a semantic actor.

*Amendment:* approximate context must be requested, not discovered. See §9 (ADA-0004) for the concrete restructure.

### 2.3 The compute saving of the deep BlockStore is assumed, never stated · **HIGH / EXPERIMENT**

This is the load-bearing one. Work through what `materialize` actually costs at depth 5.

The cached artifact is the hidden state after layer 4. For a live query to attend to that context, layers 5–15 must run over **the entire cached context**, because layers 5, 8, 10, 12, 14 are attention and need K/V for those positions. So:

```text
depth-D hidden-state cache:
  storage         = 4 KiB/token          (hidden 2048 × f16, constant in D)
  materialization = (16 − D)/16 of prefill compute, every request
  savings         ≈ D/16 of prefill
```

At D=5 that is **~31% of prefill saved, in exchange for an approximation**. At D=2 it is 12.9%, exact. Meanwhile:

```text
exact prefix ContextCheckpoint (KV + conv snapshot):
  storage         = 12 KiB/token   (6 attn layers × 8 KV heads × 64 × 2 × f16)
                    + ~120 KiB one-off conv window
  materialization = ~0 (bind and continue)
  savings         ≈ 100% of prefill, exactly
  limitation      = prefix-ordered only (linear/trie, not arbitrary subsets)
```

So the true bet the architecture is making is:

> **We will accept an approximation, and pay 69% of the prefill anyway, in order to compose cached context in arbitrary order and arbitrary subsets.**

That may well be the right bet — it's the RAG-shaped use case, and prefix checkpoints genuinely cannot do it. But it is a bet, and the pack never states it, never bounds it, and never compares it against the two obvious neighbours:

| Strategy | Storage/token | Materialize cost | Savings | Exact? | Composes arbitrarily? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact depth-2 rep | 4 KiB | 87% of prefill | 12.9% | yes (mathematically) | yes |
| Depth-D hidden rep | 4 KiB | (16−D)/16 | D/16 | no, for D≥3 | yes |
| Per-block KV, all layers ("block-isolated") | 12 KiB | ~0 | ~100% | no | yes |
| Exact prefix checkpoint | 12 KiB | ~0 | ~100% | yes | **no** — prefix only |

Note row 3: if you are accepting block-isolation as an approximation anyway, **precomputing each block's KV at every layer saves ~100% instead of 31%, for 3× the storage.** Depth-5 is a point on a continuum, not a design; it is the point where you pay two thirds of the compute to recover some cross-block attention quality.

*Amendment:* reframe depth as the explicit dial it is — see §9 (ADA-0004). The `5 → 8 → 10 → 12` sweep in the experiment note is already sweeping the right axis; the architecture doc just doesn't know that's what it is.

*Experiment (highest priority in the pack):* for D ∈ {2, 5, 8, 12, 16} and a fixed multi-block context, measure **(a)** wall-clock materialize+prefill vs. cold prefill, **(b)** quality on your multi-hop/conflicting-facts sets, **(c)** VRAM. Add row 3 (full per-block KV, D=16) and row 4 (exact prefix checkpoint) as the two boundary conditions. **Decision criterion:** if D=16 block-isolated KV is within ~1 quality point of D=5 on your task set, D=5 is strictly dominated and the hidden-state cache should be retired in favour of KV-level caching. If D=5 is materially better, you have justified the middle of the curve and should say so in ADA-0004.

### 2.4 "The first two conv layers are local, therefore blocks compose" — true, but the invariant is stated in terms of *depth* rather than *receptive field* · **MEDIUM**

The correct invariant is model-independent and the pack's version isn't:

> A block-local cached representation is exactly composable **iff** its computation graph contains no operator with unbounded receptive field, and the repair window equals Σ(kᵢ − 1) over all included cross-position operators.

For LFM2.5: repair window = 2×(3−1) = **4 tokens**. That yields two corollaries the pack is missing, both of which are live bug classes:

- **Blocks shorter than the repair window break the repair.** If a block is 3 tokens long, correct repair reaches into the block *before* its predecessor. An implementation that repairs "the boundary between adjacent blocks" is silently wrong for short blocks. Either enforce a minimum block length > repair window, or define repair recursively over predecessors.
- **Sequence-initial blocks are a different object.** The receptive-field argument assumes zero-padding at true position 0. A block cached as sequence-initial is not valid mid-sequence and vice versa — a silent 4-token error at the seam. This bites immediately, because the reference model is an *Instruct* model and every real request begins with a chat-template BOS.

Also worth pinning down precisely: "depth 2" is ambiguous between *after the conv operator*, *after the MLP*, and *after the residual add*. All three are valid cache boundaries (MLP and residual are pointwise), but a Rust reimplementation will pick a different one than the TS reference did unless it's written down. **LOW**, but free to fix now and expensive to debug later.

### 2.5 Two independent sources of approximation at depth 5 are conflated into one · **MEDIUM**

The pack says depth-5 is approximate "because layer 2 is global attention." There are actually two:

1. **Isolated attention at layer 2** — each block attended only to itself. Under RoPE this is at least *relatively* consistent (a block attending to itself uses correct relative offsets), which is why it works at all. Worth stating: RoPE relativity is the reason, and it is a property of the model you are depending on.
2. **Unrepairable conv boundaries at layers 3–4** — these are local operators, so ordinarily repairable, but their repair needs layer-2 output for the predecessor block, which was computed in isolation. So the conv discontinuity at depth 5 cannot be repaired even though the same discontinuity at depth 2 can.

These scale differently with block count and have different mitigations. Conflating them means a depth sweep will produce a quality curve you can't attribute.

**Related, and a concrete falsifiable prediction:** every independently-precomputed block has its own position-0 token, which in attention layers tends to acquire an outsized activation norm (the attention-sink phenomenon). Compose 100 blocks and you have 100 sinks in the layer-5+ KV. **Prediction: depth-5 quality degrades non-linearly in block count, not gracefully** — which is exactly the regime your "4 → 16 → 32 → 100" stress test will enter.

*Experiment (cheap, ~1 hour):* dump the L2-norm of each block's first-token hidden state at depth 5, versus the same token's norm in an ordinary joint prefill. **Decision criterion:** if the isolated norm is >2× the joint norm, prepend a shared sink/BOS prefix to every block at precompute time and drop it at composition, and re-run the block-count stress. This single mitigation may be the difference between the 100-block test working and not, and it costs almost nothing to try.

### 2.6 The WQ4 format is assumed to be a compression win; on the published numbers it is a loss · **HIGH**

Current: 32 weights → 4×u32 nibbles + 1×i32 power-of-two exponent = **20 bytes = 5.0 bpw**, with a power-of-two scale.

GGUF `Q4_0` is: 32 weights → 16 bytes of nibbles + **f16** scale = **18 bytes = 4.5 bpw**, same `[-8,7]` signed range.

So WQ4 is currently **11% larger and has strictly coarser scales** than the format it was presumably meant to improve on. The power-of-two scale costs up to a full bit of effective precision in the worst case (when max|w| sits just above a power of two, only half the quantization range is used), for a dequant saving that is negligible on a GPU where an f16 multiply is free.

Three concrete consequences:

- Replacing the i32 exponent with an f16 scale gives **18 bytes/block, better accuracy, and ~10% less decode bandwidth** (scales currently consume 1 of the 5 bits per weight; f16 halves that). That directly answers your open question *"Is one exponent per 32 weights sufficient for quality?"* — the block size is probably fine, the *scale representation* is the problem.
- **20 bytes is a 5-word stride.** For a WebGPU storage buffer, a 5-`u32` stride is a coalescing hazard — adjacent threads read misaligned. **Split into two planes**: a nibble plane (16 B/block, naturally 4-word aligned) and a separate scale plane. This makes the disk layout identical to the GPU layout, removes any repacking step, and answers *"Does repacking to an intermediate GPU-friendly layout offset compression gains?"* — with two planes, there is nothing to repack.
- Therefore: **WQ4's justification must be measured kernel throughput, not compression or quality**, because on both of those axes it currently loses to a format you can already read. Say so in the experiment note, or the format will be defended on the wrong grounds.

### 2.7 "Matmul is good enough / not the main blocker" is a subjective judgment where a roofline number exists · **MEDIUM / EXPERIMENT**

Decode reads ~1.17 B weights/token ⇒ **731 MB/token at 5 bpw** (2.34 GB at F16). The recorded 52–57 tok/s baseline is therefore ~40 GB/s of achieved bandwidth. On any desktop GPU that is a small fraction of peak, which means decode was **latency/occupancy-bound, not bandwidth-bound** — and the experiment note's premise ("primarily limited by useful memory bandwidth") was not yet true at that measurement.

Two consequences that matter architecturally, not just for kernel tuning:

- If decode is not bandwidth-bound there is **idle GPU time per token**, which makes a full-vocabulary GPU-side constraint mask essentially free (see §3.3).
- **Improving matmul makes structured generation harder.** A fixed ~1–2 ms host sync is a 6–11% regression at 18 ms/token and a **~30% regression at 6.7 ms/token.** ADA-0005 and the matmul note are coupled and neither says so.

*Experiment:* state achieved bandwidth as a fraction of `device peak` for the current kernel. **Decision criterion:** below ~50% of roofline, format compression is not the lever and further WQ4 work should stop in favour of occupancy/scheduling; above ~70%, compression is the lever and the f16-scale change in 2.6 buys real throughput.

## 3. Hidden coupling and abstraction leaks

### 3.1 `materialize` means two different things, one of which is nearly free and one of which is most of a forward pass · **HIGH**

`architecture.md` §6:

```text
materialize required Context State
  ├── reuse exact state             ← bind buffers, ~0
  ├── repair local boundaries       ← O(4 tokens × 2 layers), ~0
  ├── restore cached representation ← O(context × 11 layers)  ⚠
  └── recompute on miss             ← O(context × 16 layers)
```

Four siblings in a flow diagram, spanning four orders of magnitude of cost. This is the clean-abstraction-hiding-an-expensive-implementation case, and it is the reason 2.3 went unnoticed. Split the verb: **`bind`** (exact state, ~0), **`repair`** (bounded, receptive-field-sized), **`replay`** (run remaining layers over cached context, O(N·L)), **`recompute`** (full prefill).

### 3.2 The decode submission window is owned by nobody · **HIGH**

Three documents describe three faces of one mechanism:

- ADA-0003: "generation is GPU-resident and does not require host participation between tokens."
- ADA-0001: cancellation, disconnect, backpressure listed under *Negative consequences*, deferred.
- ADA-0005: "the primary risk is the mandatory synchronization/dependency chain."

If decode submits **D** steps ahead with no host participation, then D simultaneously determines: throughput (it hides host latency), **cancellation latency** (you cannot abort a submitted dispatch in WebGPU; at best you make it a no-op via a flag buffer written *between* submissions), **stop-sequence and EOS latency**, **max-tokens enforcement**, and **the validator's available sync budget**. These are not four problems.

Two things follow that must be stated now:

- **All host-observable control is quantized to D.** Cancel latency ≈ D tokens. Choosing D is choosing your cancellation SLA.
- **Tokens generated past a stop point must be discarded before emission, never emitted-then-retracted.** Otherwise ADA-0001's "token events are reliable request output" is false — the stream becomes non-monotonic and every client must implement retraction.

*Amendment:* promote `D` to a named runtime parameter in ADA-0003 with the invariant above, and remove cancellation from ADA-0001's deferred list — the *transport* of a cancel message is deferrable, its *granularity* is not.

### 3.3 Structured generation's dependency chain is being priced without the price · **HIGH / EXPERIMENT**

ADA-0005 is well-reasoned but every candidate is ranked against a cost nobody has measured. The measurement is available today and needs no validator:

*Experiment (≈30 lines, do this first):*

1. Measure the distribution (p50/p99) of `queue.submit → mapAsync resolve → next submit` for a 4-byte buffer in your actual Chrome/Dawn build.
2. Measure achieved decode tok/s with a forced per-token 4-byte readback and **no validator logic at all**.

**Decision criterion:** if forced-sync decode retains ≥85% of unconstrained tok/s, **candidate A wins and candidate B should not be built** — a CPU validator with GPU mask upload is simpler, more testable, and grammar-general. Below ~70%, GPU-resident validation is mandatory and the extra complexity is justified.

**And a correction to the pack's framing of candidate B.** ADA-0005 lists "memory growth" and "preprocessing complexity" as B's risks. Those risks come from assuming a **token-level** transition table (|states| × 65536 entries — hundreds of MB, correctly rejected). At the **byte** level the problem dissolves:

- Byte-DFA transition table: |states| × 256 × u32 ≈ **1 MB for 1000 states**.
- Token→bytes table: flat byte array + offsets ≈ **400 KB** for the whole vocabulary.
- Per decode step, recompute the full mask from scratch: **65,536 threads, each walking its token's ≤16 bytes through the DFA.** That is ~16 dependent lookups per lane into an L2-resident table. Microseconds — and per 2.7 you likely have idle GPU time to absorb it.

So the mask never needs to be transported, because it is cheaper to recompute than to transfer. State transition after sampling is the same walk on one token. **The decode loop stays fully GPU-resident with zero host sync**, which would be a genuinely strong result — and it is not the design ADA-0005 currently describes under B.

The constraint is that the grammar must be regular or bounded-stack. JSON Schema with bounded nesting depth is fine (encode the stack in a few `u32`s of validator state); unbounded recursion is not. Given ADA-0005's own advice to start with `boolean enum / small string enum / fixed object / integer range`, all of your first targets are regular.

*Amendment:* add this as **candidate F — GPU byte-DFA with per-step full-vocabulary mask recomputation**, and note explicitly that mask *recomputation* may be cheaper than mask *transport*.

### 3.4 Candidate D is not "risky" — it is exactly wrong for sampling and exactly right for greedy · **MEDIUM**

ADA-0005 says top-K-before-validation "may alter constrained sampling semantics." Sharper:

- Correct constrained sampling is `top_k(mask(logits))`. Candidate D computes `mask(top_k(logits))`. These differ **whenever a masked-out token is in the top K** — i.e. almost always, not just in the pathological empty-set case. For any stochastic sampler, D is not a risk, it is incorrect.
- **For greedy argmax, D is exactly correct**: return the top-K sorted, take the first valid one, escalate K if all K are invalid. Since your current sampler *is* greedy, this is a legitimate exact fast path today — 128 bytes of readback instead of 8 KiB — and it gives you a correctness oracle for the mask path.

Worth writing down because it means the correctness of D is a function of the sampler, and someone will later swap greedy for top-p and silently break it.

### 3.5 `CacheStats` is classified as droppable telemetry, but under the depth-5 design "which cache class served this request" is a result property · **MEDIUM**

ADA-0001 says telemetry may be throttled/dropped. Fine for `EngineStats`. But if approximate context can serve a request, then **whether it did** is part of the answer, not observation of it. Same for `SamplingTrace` if you ever want to reproduce a generation. Split the reliable subset (cache class used, seed, isolation depth, model/tokenizer identity hash) into the terminal `GenerationFinished` event.

### 3.6 The runtime API promises multi-client; the Context State design is single-instance · **HIGH**

`references.md` records `kvBuffer` and `convBuffer` — singular. ADA-0001 promises a daemon serving CLI, editor, browser, and batch clients concurrently, and says "persistent BlockStore becomes naturally shareable across clients."

Two concurrent generations need two Context States. At 12 KiB/token × 32k that is **384 MiB of KV per session**, on top of ~800 MB of weights. So the options are: serialize (which is a scheduler — explicitly a §3 non-goal), allocate per-session (VRAM-bounded to a small number of sessions), or share read-only cached context with per-session live state.

You do not need to solve scheduling now. You need to **name `Session` as the owner of Context State**, and state that concurrency across sessions is currently serialized. That is a one-paragraph decision that prevents a painful retrofit, and it makes the VRAM budget an explicit, per-session number instead of a global surprise.

### 3.7 BlockStore sharing across clients is a data-exposure surface · **HIGH**

Local, not distributed — this is in scope. A resident daemon plus a browser that can reach it means any page can drive your engine. If block IDs are global and guessable, a client can reference another client's cached context — which may be a private document — and receive answers grounded in it. "Naturally shareable across clients" is stated as a *Positive* consequence in ADA-0001 without a namespace.

*Invariant:* **blocks are namespaced by an owner principal; cross-namespace reference requires explicit sharing.** The transport-level answer (unix socket vs. loopback TCP vs. handshake token) stays deferred; the namespacing invariant should not.

### 3.8 ADA-0006's decision trigger depends on an artifact ADA-0001 declines to produce · **LOW**

Trigger 1 is "Web UI communicates only through the logical runtime API," but ADA-0001's non-decisions include the public message schema. Not a contradiction (logical API ≠ wire schema), but the trigger is unfalsifiable as written. Resolve by producing the **logical** API — commands, events, identities, lifecycle — as a typed interface, with framing and transport still deferred. That also happens to be the artifact that makes the eventual Rust port a port rather than a rewrite.

## 4. Likely performance traps

### 4.1 KV cannot live in one buffer at the stated context length · **HIGH**

12 KiB/token × 32,768 = **384 MiB**. WebGPU's default `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` is **128 MiB** and default `maxBufferSize` is **256 MiB**. Desktop Chrome will usually *grant* higher limits on request, but the design must not silently depend on that.

Per-layer KV buffers give 32,768 × 2 KiB = **64 MiB per attention layer** — comfortably inside defaults. This is not a kernel detail: BlockStore residency accounting, checkpoint restore, spill, and eviction all address KV, and they must address it in units that respect the binding limit.

*Amendment:* make per-layer (or otherwise chunked) KV an explicit architectural invariant in ADA-0003, with the maximum context length derived from `min(granted binding size, VRAM budget)` and **reported by the runtime**, not assumed from the model card. The pack currently carries "32,768-token stated context length" from the model card and separately says "current runtime limits remain implementation-dependent" — that gap is where the trap lives.

### 4.2 Rollback on a hybrid model is not a pointer rewind · **HIGH**

In a pure transformer, any prefix length is a free checkpoint: KV for a prefix *is* a prefix of the KV array. In LFM2.5 that is false. The conv rolling window at position *t* is a per-layer intermediate that is not otherwise retained, so **only positions where you explicitly snapshotted conv windows are restorable.** The set of valid checkpoint positions is discrete and equals the set of positions you chose in advance.

The snapshot is cheap — 10 conv layers × 3 × 2048 × f16 ≈ **120 KiB per position**, so every 64 tokens costs ~1.9 KiB/token amortized against 12 KiB/token of KV. But it must be designed in, and right now nothing in the pack requires it.

This is the sharpest hybrid-specific consequence in the whole design, and it couples ADA-0004 to ADA-0005: **a validator that backtracks on a dead end needs a Context State rollback, which needs conv snapshots.** ADA-0005 requires "deterministic dead-end behavior"; ADA-0004 lists "how future sampling/validation state participates in checkpoint identity" as an open question. They are the same question and it is more urgent than its placement suggests.

*Mitigation that avoids the problem entirely:* **trim the grammar automaton to co-accessible states** — remove every state from which no accepting state is reachable. Then no valid token can lead to a dead end by construction, and the only remaining dead-end source is a max-length cutoff. Do this and you may never need rollback for structured generation at all. Say so in ADA-0005; it converts a hard runtime requirement into a compile-time one.

### 4.3 Cold-request latency cliff has no API surface · **MEDIUM**

`recompute on miss` sits as a peer of `reuse exact state` in the flow. For a 32k context those differ by ~10 seconds. Any client that does not know which it is getting will set the wrong timeout. Surface an estimate (tokens requiring recompute) at request-accept time, before the first token event.

### 4.4 Reliable token stream + slow consumer + GPU-resident decode is over-constrained · **MEDIUM**

ADA-0001 says token events must not be silently dropped; ADA-0003 says decode must not require host participation. If a consumer stalls, the only options are unbounded buffering, blocking decode (which violates ADA-0003), or dropping (which violates ADA-0001). The resolution has to be stated: **bounded buffer; overflow terminates the request with an explicit error; decode never blocks; tokens are never silently dropped.** Same for disconnect — default to cancelling on disconnect unless the request declared itself detachable, or one closed browser tab pins the GPU indefinitely.

### 4.5 Masked-logit numerics · **MEDIUM**

Two traps in one kernel:

- In f16, a "large negative" sentinel like −1e9 is not representable and saturates to −inf. Usually harmless, but if **every** token is masked, the softmax produces NaN and you sample garbage instead of detecting a dead end. **Dead-end detection must be an explicit GPU-computed flag (`allowed_count == 0`) surfaced as a distinct terminal event**, not an emergent property of the arithmetic.
- **Order is semantics:** `mask → temperature → top-k/top-p → sample`. Any other order changes the distribution. If you add repetition penalties or other logit processors later, their position in that pipeline is observable and must be specified, not incidental.

### 4.6 Non-canonical tokenization under constraints · **MEDIUM**

A byte-level grammar admits many token sequences for the same string. Masking at token level lets the model emit `"` + `na` + `me` where the canonical tokenization is `"name` — legal output, but off-distribution for the model, and it degrades quality in a way that reads exactly like a bug. Related: when a constrained region begins mid-token-boundary, the first constrained token needs token healing. ADA-0005's "token bytes/text interpretation" bullet is too coarse to cover either. Both belong in the reference-validator spec.

### 4.7 Tokenizer byte mapping is a shared prerequisite nobody owns · **MEDIUM**

Both the CPU and GPU validator paths need the same artifact: **the decoded byte expansion of every token id, as a flat byte array plus offsets**, produced by the same detokenizer used for output. Get this wrong — GPT-2-style `Ġ` remapping treated as a literal character, byte-fallback `<0xNN>` tokens, special/chat-template tokens like `<|im_start|>` — and every validator design fails identically. Build it and its conformance test *before* choosing between A, B, C, D, or F; it is the one artifact common to all of them.

## 5. Decisions frozen too early

### 5.1 "GPU-resident decode fast path — ACCEPTED / High" as an absolute · **MEDIUM-HIGH**

Stated as an invariant ("new features should not silently regress this property") before the cost of violating it has been measured. The risk is not that the invariant is wrong — it is that as an *absolute*, it will push ADA-0005 toward a complex GPU validator to protect a benefit that might be worth 5%.

*Reframe as a budget:* "constrained and instrumented decode must retain ≥85% of unconstrained tok/s; no feature may introduce a host roundtrip that idles the GPU **within** a submission window." That preserves the real property (no serializing stall), is measurable, and does not pre-decide ADA-0005. Then run §3.3's experiment and either re-freeze at the absolute or keep the budget.

### 5.2 `CachedRepresentation` with an `exactnessClass` field · **HIGH**

Discussed in 2.2 — the taxonomy is PROVISIONAL in ADA-0004 but its vocabulary has already propagated into `architecture.md` §5 as though settled. Fix before it reaches the API.

### 5.3 "Recent matmul work indicates performance is good enough" · **MEDIUM**

A subjective judgment recorded as a direction-setting conclusion, where a roofline fraction is computable (2.7). Keep the *decision* (don't let matmul block architecture work) — replace the *justification* with the number.

## 6. Decisions that should probably be frozen now

Each of these is cheap today and expensive to retrofit. None of them requires resolving anything currently marked OPEN.

1. **`Session` owns Context State.** Concurrency across sessions is serialized for now. VRAM budget is stated per session. *(closes 3.6)*
2. **Cache identity = hash(model weight bytes ‖ quantization format+version ‖ tokenizer files ‖ kernel-semantics version).** The last term matters: depth-2 hidden states computed under WQ4 revision A are numerically different from revision B, and mixing them within one composition is a silent inconsistency with no symptom. *(closes a 2.1-class bug)*
3. **The runtime's input is token IDs.** Text→tokens is a convenience layer; the chat template is an explicit, versioned step whose output is inspectable. Otherwise block reuse across chat turns is unsound the first time you template a cached document. *(follows from ADA-0004 invariant 3, which currently only gestures at it)*
4. **Token IDs are retained for any block with a live logical identity; only derived representations are evictable.** Without this, an evicted block whose owner disconnected is unrecoverable and "recompute on miss" is false. *(closes a hole in ADA-0004 invariant 2)*
5. **Decode submission window `D` is a named runtime parameter**, and all host-observable control (cancel, stop sequences, max tokens, validator sync) is quantized to it. Tokens past a stop point are discarded before emission. *(closes 3.2)*
6. **Token-event reliability rule:** bounded buffer; overflow terminates the request with an error; decode never blocks; nothing is silently dropped. Disconnect cancels unless the request is detachable. *(closes 4.4)*
7. **Determinism contract + numeric oracle.** See 8.1 — this is the prerequisite for ADA-0007 being executable at all.
8. **Approximate context is a request mode carrying an isolation depth, not a cache tier**, and the mode used is reported in the terminal event. *(closes 2.2 and 3.5)*
9. **WGSL lives in standalone `.wgsl` assets specialized via pipeline-overridable constants — no host-language shader codegen.** TypeScript template-literal shader generation is the single most common thing that turns a Rust port into a rewrite, and it is invisible until you attempt the port.
10. **The compiled grammar IR — not the schema language — is the TS↔core boundary.** ADA-0006 already flags schema-pop/ArkType as a TS-native coupling risk; ADA-0005 already says the public format need not be the internal one. Naming the IR as the seam resolves both at once and lets TS own schema→IR permanently.
11. **WQ4 container gets a tensor directory, a format version, and a hash of the source GGUF.** Today the loader binds tensors by *position in GGUF iteration order* — an implicit, unversioned contract with another library's traversal. Any change on either side silently mis-binds tensors and produces plausible-but-wrong output. That is precisely the failure class ADA-0007 exists to prevent, and the fix is an afternoon.
12. **Block namespacing by owner principal.** *(closes 3.7)*

## 7. Open questions correctly deferred

No objection to any of these, and I'd resist pressure to close them early:

- Transport, framing, socket type, wire schema (ADA-0001 non-decisions) — correctly deferred, *provided* the logical API is written down (3.8).
- Host language (ADA-0006) — the reasoning is right and the trigger list is right. One caveat: the deferral has a hidden expiry. Every month of TS growth adds port cost, and all four triggers are "when things stabilize," which can fail to fire indefinitely. Add a negative trigger: *if a native daemon becomes required before triggers 1–3 fire, port the core boundary only and leave experiments in TS.*
- Eviction policy, host spill tier, block groups, semantic block roles, max block count (block-cache "do not freeze yet") — correct, and the ordering (understand GPU-resident behavior first) is right.
- Optimal exact and approximate cache depths — correct, and 2.3 sharpens the sweep rather than closing it.
- Public grammar/schema representation — correct, given 6.10.
- Second model family (ADA-0002) — correct. "Genericity is not a milestone" should stay.
- Sampler placement (ADA-0005) — correctly open, and 3.4 means greedy gives you a usable exact path in the meantime.

## 8. Missing invariants

### 8.1 Determinism contract and numeric-equivalence oracle · **HIGH — prerequisite for ADA-0007**

ADA-0007's entire strategy is differential testing against a reference path. That silently assumes the two paths are comparable by output equality, which per 2.1 they are not. Without a stated contract you cannot distinguish a composition bug from float reassociation, and every experiment result in the pack inherits that ambiguity.

State:

> **Determinism:** identical runtime build + identical device + identical inputs + identical cache class ⇒ bit-identical logits. (Verify — split-K reductions and atomics can break this; if the argmax reduction uses atomics, ties are already nondeterministic.)
>
> **Cross-path equivalence:** cached and uncached paths are equivalent *up to floating-point reassociation*, measured as **max |Δlogit|**, **top-1 agreement rate** over a corpus, and **divergence-onset position** — not string equality.

The block-cache experiment's "1:1 output for tested prompts" should be restated in these terms, and its metric list extended. This is cheap and it makes every subsequent BlockStore experiment trustworthy.

### 8.2 Composability invariant stated over receptive field, not depth · **MEDIUM**

Per 2.4, with the short-block and sequence-initial corollaries as explicit rules.

### 8.3 Checkpoint positions are discrete · **HIGH**

Per 4.2. A `ContextCheckpoint` is valid only at positions where conv windows were snapshotted; the snapshot interval is a runtime parameter; rollback is only possible to those positions.

### 8.4 GC and eviction cannot alter an in-flight request · **MEDIUM**

`GarbageCollect` appears as an engine command. A generation must pin its resolved materialization for its lifetime. Otherwise memory pressure becomes a semantic actor mid-stream.

### 8.5 Context-limit overflow policy · **MEDIUM-HIGH**

`SetContextLimit` is filed with engine lifecycle, but "what happens when a request exceeds the limit" is a semantic policy: reject, truncate (which silently changes meaning), or slide. On a hybrid model, sliding and truncation are *not* free — both need conv state at the new boundary (4.2). Pick reject-by-default now; it is the only option that cannot silently change an answer.

### 8.6 Sequence-position-0 uniqueness · **MEDIUM**

Exactly one block may occupy position 0; cached representations record whether they were computed as sequence-initial. Per 2.4, this bites immediately with the Instruct chat template.

### 8.7 Idle residency policy · **LOW-MEDIUM**

A daemon holding ~800 MB of VRAM forever is hostile on a shared desktop. An unload-after-idle policy interacts with 6.2: after unload/reload, cached representations remain valid **iff** the identity hash matches — which is another reason to freeze the hash content now.

## If development continued for a month on this specification, the three most likely regrets

### 1. That "cache is an optimization" was written down as settled when it is already false in both implemented paths

It reads as the safest sentence in the pack. It is the riskiest. Depth-2 is not bit-exact, so warm and cold caches can produce different text; depth-5 has no exact path to fall back to, so eviction is a semantic event. A month from now you will have a corpus of BlockStore experiments — depth sweeps, permutations, multi-hop, conflicting facts — whose only oracle was output equality, and no way to separate "the composition is wrong" from "the floats reassociated" from "the approximation degraded." The results won't be *wrong* so much as **uninterpretable**, and re-running them is the expensive part. The fix costs an afternoon today: state the determinism contract, define the numeric oracle, and re-label depth-5 as a mode.

### 2. That `materialize` in §6 was one word instead of four, so nobody priced the deep BlockStore

Depth-5 saves roughly D/L ≈ 31% of prefill, in exchange for an approximation, when an exact prefix checkpoint saves ~100% and full block-isolated KV saves ~100% for 3× storage. The pack names `ContextCheckpoint` and then never operationalizes it — no experiment, no invariant, no place in the request flow — while the approximate path gets the depth sweep and the stress tests. A month of tuning depth, block size, and eviction on the 31% path is a month not spent on the mechanism that is both faster and exact for the case that probably dominates real usage (a long, stable, *ordered* prefix). The BlockStore bet may still be right — arbitrary-order composition is a real capability that checkpoints cannot provide — but you'd be making it on purpose instead of by default.

### 3. That the GPU-resident decode invariant, cancellation latency, and the validator's sync budget are one parameter wearing three documents' clothes

ADA-0003 froze "no host participation between tokens" as ACCEPTED/High before anyone measured what a host roundtrip actually costs in your Chrome build. That freeze will quietly steer ADA-0005 toward the most complex candidate to defend a property whose value is unknown — and it will keep steering for the whole month, because each individual decision will look locally justified. Meanwhile the same unnamed parameter sets your cancellation SLA, which ADA-0001 has filed under "Negative consequences, to be resolved later," and it is on the critical path of the *only* two experiments that can discriminate between validator architectures. Two measurements — the map-round-trip distribution, and forced-sync decode throughput with no validator at all — take an afternoon and would let the next month's validator work be a decision rather than a bet.
